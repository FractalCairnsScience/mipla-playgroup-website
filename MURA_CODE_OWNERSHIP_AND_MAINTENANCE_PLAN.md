# Mura Playgroup website: proposed code ownership and maintenance plan

## Purpose

This plan supports the requested move to a separate Mura Playgroup website while keeping the service maintainable by Mura. It is a proposed operational arrangement for review by Mura’s CEO and nominated website administrator; it does not itself transfer any account, domain, licence, or intellectual-property right.

## Proposed ownership model

The recommended arrangement is that **Mura Kosker Sorority Inc.** is the service owner, domain registrant, and administrator of the final source-code repository and hosting account. The Playgroup website should be owned by an organisation-controlled account rather than an individual staff member’s personal account.

| Area | Proposed owner | Day-to-day role | Required confirmation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website content and cultural approval | Mura Kosker Sorority Inc. | CEO and nominated content approver | Name the authorised approver and backup contact. |
| Domain name and DNS | Mura Kosker Sorority Inc. | Nominated Mura administrator | Confirm the domain to use and the billing contact. |
| Source code repository | Mura-controlled GitHub organisation or private repository | Two nominated Mura administrators; developer access as required | Confirm the organisation/account that will own the repository. |
| Hosting and deployment | Mura-controlled hosting account | Nominated Mura administrator | Choose the final hosting account and keep at least two organisation administrators. |
| Technical maintenance | Mura-appointed maintainer | Apply approved fixes, updates and backups | Confirm maintenance contact, approval path, and response expectations. |

## Handover checklist

Before launch under Mura control, the website team should create or nominate the Mura-controlled repository, hosting account, and domain account. Two authorised Mura staff members should have administrator access to each account. The existing repository should then be transferred or mirrored to that organisation-controlled location, with a release tag created for the handover version.

The handover pack should include the source code, this plan, the facilitator guide, a short deployment guide, a list of external services, and an asset register. The asset register must identify the source and approval status of every logo, photograph, video, illustration, and downloadable document. Cultural content and images must not be updated without the approved Mura review process.

## Maintenance workflow

Routine changes should be requested through the nominated Mura contact, reviewed for content and cultural suitability, tested in a preview environment, and approved by Mura before publication. Urgent security or availability fixes may be applied by the technical maintainer, with the Mura contact advised as soon as practicable.

| Change type | Approval required before release | Evidence retained |
|---|---|---|
| Text, links, event dates, downloadable documents | Nominated content approver | Request and approval in the change record. |
| Cultural stories, language, images, videos, logos | Nominated cultural/content approver | Asset source, usage approval, and final approval. |
| Functional changes to planners or forms | Content approver and technical maintainer | Test result and release note. |
| Hosting, domain, or access changes | CEO or authorised delegate | Account change record and updated access register. |

## Access, backups, and continuity

Access should follow the least-privilege principle. Credentials must be kept in the Mura-controlled account or approved password-management system, never in source code, documents, or personal email. The nominated administrators should review access when staff or contractors change roles.

At each approved release, the maintainer should keep a tagged source-code copy and downloadable handover archive in the Mura-controlled repository. The live site configuration, domain renewal details, asset register, and current administrator contacts should be reviewed at least annually and after any change of CEO, website administrator, hosting provider, or domain registrar.

## Decisions still required from Mura

Mura needs to nominate the final code-repository owner, hosting-account owner, domain, two administrators, content/cultural approver, and technical-maintenance contact. Once those details are confirmed, the proposed model above can be converted into the final handover record and the service can be published from the Mura-controlled account.
